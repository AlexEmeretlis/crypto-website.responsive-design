From Trading Screens to Code Editors: Why I'm Building in Fintech

Five years staring at candlestick charts taught me more about product design than any UX course could.

I spent those years scalping, day trading, and running swing positions — all through a professional terminal connected directly to the exchange via API keys. Every second mattered. Every interface decision, every delay in loading a chart, every extra click between seeing an opportunity and acting on it — it all had a real cost, measured in real money.

That's when I started noticing something most traders never think about: the tools themselves. Why did some platforms feel instant, while others felt like wading through mud during a fast market? Why did some dashboards surface the right information at the right moment, while others buried it three menus deep?

I wasn't thinking like a developer yet. I was thinking like a user — a demanding one, shaped by years of needing information now, not in three seconds.

Eventually, curiosity won. I wanted to understand what was actually happening behind that terminal. How did the API calls work? What made one interface faster than another? Could I build something like this myself?

So I started learning to code. From zero. HTML, CSS, JavaScript — the fundamentals, built the slow way, one project at a time.

I'm not leaving trading behind. I'm bringing it with me. Every project I build now is shaped by five years of understanding what traders actually need: speed, clarity, and interfaces that get out of the way.

This is where those two worlds meet — the trader who knows what the tool should do, and the developer learning how to build it.
